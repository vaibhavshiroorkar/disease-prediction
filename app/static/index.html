About

An open machine learning project. It shows what these models can and cannot tell you.

How to read a result

A risk check gives a score from 0 to 100%. During training, each model picked a cutoff. A score above the cutoff counts as flagged.

Each cutoff trades missed cases against false alarms. The dengue model sets its cutoff low so it catches 90 in 100 cases that turn serious. The cost is that only 3 in 100 of its flags turn serious. Every result states these numbers for its own model.

The symptom checker works differently. It ranks the conditions in its training data that share your symptoms. Its scores compare conditions with each other. They are not the chance that you have one.

What the models cannot do

  • Diagnose anyone. They learned from small public datasets, not from your medical history.
  • Speak for other populations. The diabetes model, for example, only saw Pima women aged 21 and over.
  • Explain causes. An input that moved a score shows how the model behaves, not what causes disease.
  • Read a whole slide or a test strip. The malaria model reads one cropped cell.

When you leave an optional measurement blank, the model fills it with the training median. The result lists every value it filled in.
