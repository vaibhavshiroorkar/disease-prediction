About

What a result means, and what these models cannot tell you.

How to read a result

A risk check returns a score from 0 to 100%. Each model has a cutoff picked during training. A score at or above it is flagged.

Cutoffs trade missed cases against false alarms. Most of these models set theirs low so they miss few real cases, which means many flags turn out to be false alarms. Each result says how often that happened on the test data.

The symptom checker works differently. It ranks the conditions in its training data that share your symptoms. Its scores compare those conditions with each other. They are not the chance that you have any of them.

What the models cannot do

  • Diagnose anyone. They learned from public research datasets, not from your history.
  • Speak for other populations. The diabetes model only saw Pima women aged 21 and over.
  • Explain causes. An input that moved a score shows how the model behaves, not what causes disease.
  • Read a whole slide or a test strip. The malaria model reads one cropped cell.

When you leave an input blank, the model fills it with a typical training value: the median for measurements, the most common answer for yes/no questions. The result lists every value it filled in.
